Research: Condition and Effect on Nigerian Organizations and Homes

Fuel subsidy removal and its impact on transport — documented through the voices of Nigerian organizations, families, schools, and civil society.

Methodology

How we heard from Nigerians across the country.

  • 1

    Anonymous 3-minute bus stop intercept at key corridors: Berger, Nyanya, Kubwa — between 6am and 8am, when commuters are heading to work.

  • 2

    8-minute MDA staff survey distributed through HR departments and WhatsApp groups of targeted government and public sector offices.

  • 3

    Target sample: 250–400 respondents, stratified by corridor (Kubwa, Nyanya/Mararaba, Lugbe, Gwarinpa) and employment type.

  • 4

    Data collection: Anonymous Google Forms, with no identifying questions. All data stored in encrypted, access-controlled Google Sheets.

Key Research Questions

  • Q1

    How has fuel subsidy removal changed daily transport costs for Nigerian organizations and households?

  • Q2

    What coping strategies are being used, and where are they failing?

  • Q3

    What would make verified, affordable ride-share work for Nigerian organizations?

  • Q4

    How do transport burdens differ across corridors, income levels, and institution types?

Data Use & Protection

Your voice matters — and your privacy is protected.

  • Data is collected for research only. We do not sell or share individual responses.

  • Responses are anonymous unless you choose to volunteer contact information for follow-up.

  • You can request deletion of your data at any time by emailing olandasolutions@gmail.com.

  • All findings are published anonymized — no individual or organization is identified without explicit consent.
